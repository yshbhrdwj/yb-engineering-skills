# Privacy through the data lifecycle

Use when the scope handles personal, sensitive, inferred, or linkable data, including retirement and restored backups.

Trace actual collection, purpose, transformation, storage, use, recipients, copies, retention, and deletion. A privacy-policy statement is not evidence of implementation. Identify affected people and harm, not only fields labeled personal data.

Compare each field with its necessity and purpose. Consider smaller payloads, coarser precision, less identifiable alternatives, restricted access, and shorter retention. Distinguish anonymization from pseudonymization; assess linkability/re-identification rather than claiming guaranteed anonymity.

Inspect relevant defaults, notices, choices, and consent or other applicable processing basis in the product's jurisdiction. Do not assume every processing activity requires consent. Verify specific legal claims against current official authority and scope before asserting them.

Trace retention/deletion across primary records, indexes, caches, analytics, exports, third parties, and backup expiry. Distinguish immediate access removal, scheduled destruction, and retained backups; inspect restore-time re-deletion. Check account deletion, access/export, correction, and choice propagation against accepted product commitments and verified applicable requirements.

Review processors/subprocessors, cross-border transfers, access from other regions, and organizational/contractual dependencies only where material. Surface missing ownership or policy decisions as questions, not invented obligations. Record known retained copies, deletion limitations, and the next evidence needed; never promise immediate universal erasure without proof.
